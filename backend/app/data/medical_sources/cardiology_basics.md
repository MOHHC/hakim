# Cardiovascular Emergencies — Clinical Guide

## Chest Pain Assessment

Chest pain is one of the most common presenting complaints in emergency medicine.
It accounts for approximately 5-8% of all emergency department visits worldwide.
The differential diagnosis is broad and includes both life-threatening and benign conditions.

Key life-threatening causes include:
- Acute myocardial infarction (AMI)
- Pulmonary embolism (PE)
- Aortic dissection
- Tension pneumothorax
- Cardiac tamponade

## History Taking — SOCRATES Framework

A structured history is essential for accurate risk stratification.

**Site:** Where exactly is the pain located? Is it central, left-sided, or right-sided?

**Onset:** Was it sudden or gradual? What was the patient doing at the time?

**Character:** Describe the pain — crushing, sharp, burning, tearing, pressure-like?

**Radiation:** Does it radiate to the arm, jaw, back, or shoulder?

**Associated symptoms:** Shortness of breath, sweating, nausea, palpitations, syncope?

**Timing:** Is it constant or intermittent? How long has it been present?

**Exacerbating/relieving factors:** Does it worsen with exertion, breathing, or movement? Does rest or GTN relieve it?

**Severity:** Rate the pain on a scale of 0-10.

## Physical Examination

Vital signs must be obtained within 2 minutes of presentation.
Measure blood pressure in both arms — a difference >20 mmHg suggests aortic dissection.

Auscultate for:
- Muffled heart sounds (tamponade)
- New murmur (valve pathology or VSD post-MI)
- Bilateral crackles (pulmonary oedema in LVF)
- Absent breath sounds (pneumothorax)

Assess peripheral perfusion: capillary refill time, skin colour, temperature, and peripheral pulses.

## Investigations

### ECG
A 12-lead ECG must be performed within 10 minutes of presentation.
Look for:
- ST elevation ≥1mm in two contiguous leads → STEMI (activate cath lab immediately)
- ST depression / T-wave inversion → NSTEMI / unstable angina
- New LBBB → treat as STEMI equivalent
- Sinus tachycardia + right heart strain pattern (S1Q3T3) → consider PE

### Biomarkers
- Troponin I or T (high-sensitivity): draw on arrival and at 3 hours
- A rise and/or fall pattern with at least one value above the 99th percentile confirms myocardial injury

### Imaging
- Chest X-ray: widened mediastinum (dissection), cardiomegaly, pulmonary oedema, pneumothorax
- CT pulmonary angiography: gold standard for PE
- CT aortography: gold standard for aortic dissection
- Bedside echo: useful for tamponade, wall motion abnormalities, RV dilatation

## Acute Management

### STEMI Protocol
1. Dual antiplatelet therapy: aspirin 300mg + ticagrelor 180mg (or clopidogrel 600mg)
2. Anticoagulation: unfractionated heparin or enoxaparin
3. Primary PCI within 90 minutes of first medical contact (door-to-balloon time)
4. If PCI unavailable within 120 minutes: fibrinolysis with tenecteplase

### NSTEMI / Unstable Angina
1. Risk stratify using GRACE or TIMI score
2. Antiplatelet + anticoagulation therapy
3. Early invasive strategy (within 24-72h) for high-risk patients
4. Medical management for low-risk patients

## Triage Classification

**RED (Immediate):** Active STEMI, haemodynamic instability, suspected aortic dissection, cardiac arrest

**YELLOW (Urgent):** Suspected NSTEMI, new-onset chest pain with risk factors, troponin elevation

**GREEN (Semi-urgent):** Atypical chest pain, low GRACE score, normal ECG and initial troponin

## Common Presentations in Arabic-Speaking Patients

Patients may describe chest pain using colloquial terms:
- وجع بالصدر (waja3 bil sadr) — chest pain
- ضغط على الصدر (daght 3al sadr) — pressure on chest
- حرقة (2ar2a / 7ar2a) — burning sensation (often confused with GERD)
- قلبي بيدق (2albi bidi2) — my heart is pounding (palpitations)
- ما2dar 2itmashsha — cannot walk (exertional chest pain)
